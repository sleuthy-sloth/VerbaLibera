# Sketch: Broadsheet

## Variant: Broadsheet

### Design stance
Language learning as reading. The app is trying to make a beginner comfortable
with written French; this direction stops pretending it is a game and treats the
learner as a reader.

### Key choices
- **No cards, no glass, no shadows.** Hairline rules, a measured column, and
  paper. Depth is `none` — structure comes from rules and type scale.
- **The feedback is a margin note**, not a panel: a left rule with the model set
  as a 42px pull-quote and the explanation in italic serif beneath a hairline.
- **Options are a numbered quiz list**, not pills. The selected row gets a rule
  and a weight change, deliberately *not* a filled card.
- **A masthead and kicker** (`VERBALIBERA · FRANÇAIS A1`, `LESSON 1 OF 25`) give it
  the feel of a publication.
- **IBM Plex Mono for everything technical** — numbers, labels, the CTA.

### Trade-offs
- **Strong at:** calm, gravitas, and zero childishness. The most distinctive of
  the three and the hardest for a competitor to imitate without meaning it.
  Excellent at large text sizes and on lower-end devices — no filters at all.
- **Weak at:** it under-sells the audio and speaking work, which are the app's
  real differentiators, and it is the least "pick up for eight minutes on the
  bus" of the three. Repeated twice, it could read as austere or academic.

### Best for
- Adult self-learners, and a product positioning that competes on rigour and
  taste rather than on fun.
